**Simulations, Models, Twins** explores modes of simulative technologies and speculates on their potential for practitioners of the built environment. Digital twins, agent-based models, and other types of simulations serve a similar purpose to mock-ups and scale models in traditional architecture practices: there is utility in testing a design in a "real" environment, whether physical or simulated.

At the same time, simulations are active agents in shaping the realities they depict, and the degree of "reality" they capture is a continuous source of debate. The course emphasizes hands-on experimentation with simulative technologies as tools for crafting arguments, building worlds, and testing how proposals might perform in the "real" world, while bearing in mind that these representations are partial, situated, and constructed.

First offered in Fall 2026.
